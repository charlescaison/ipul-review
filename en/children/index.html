Let the children come

At our churches, children are not an afterthought. On Sunday, while the sermon is preached, children have a message of their own, led by a teacher, where they learn about Jesus.

  • Jesus welcomed the children
  • Why we teach children
  • Soldiers of Jesus: learning the armor of God
  • Questions about the children's message?

Jesus welcomed the children

In the Gospels, people brought their little children to Jesus so that He could bless them. His disciples tried to send them away. Jesus was not pleased with that. He called the children to Him.

This is the invitation we want every child in our church to hear: come to Jesus.

Why we teach children

God wants children to hear His word

When Moses told Israel to gather for the reading of the law, the little ones were included, so that they would hear it and learn it.

Faith is handed on from one generation to the next

God asked His people to tell their children what He had done, so that each new generation would set its hope in Him.

Come to Jesus.

Teaching begins at home, and the church stands beside it

The Bible gives parents the first place in teaching their children. The children's message is there to stand beside parents in that work, not to take their place.

What children learn early stays with them

Timothy is an example. Paul reminded him that from childhood he had known the Holy Scriptures.

Soldiers of Jesus: learning the armor of God

In one lesson, the children learned about the armor of God and showed it to the whole church. They wore a helmet, a breastplate, a shield, a belt and carried a sword, each one marked with what it stands for. They stood at the front with a sign that read "Soldado de Jesús", Soldier of Jesus.

The sword of the Spirit is the word of God. That is why we want children to learn the Bible: it is the sword God puts in their hands.
